The Life Out Of Reach series is memoir in its truest sense; no protective layers or distance, no tidy resolutions and no version of events shaped to make anyone, including me, look better than the truth allows. These books are built for exactly the kind of reading a book club does best: close, honest and willing to sit with questions that don’t have comfortable answers. Here are discussion questions for both titles:

<h2><a href="/books/spare-burden/">Spare Burden</a></h2>

<p>I grew up as one of six daughters in a Sikh Punjabi family in 1980s Britain, being constantly reminded that as a girl, I was worth less than a boy.</p>

<p>Spare Burden opens the series with my childhood and adolescence: the messages that I received as a girl about my worth, the expectations placed upon me as a daughter, the complicated boundaries between love and duty and the gradual understanding that my future may not belong entirely to me.</p>

<p>For book clubs, Spare Burden opens up questions around gender, cultural identity, immigration, family hierarchy, belonging, inherited beliefs, education, ambition and what happens when I begin to question the rules that I’d been raised to accept.</p>

<h2><a href="/books//books/not-the-property-of/">Not The Property Of</a></h2>

<p>The title is not a metaphor. It’s something I had to fight very hard to eventually make true, but it wasn’t true during the second book.

<p>At sixteen, I’m engaged. Without any notice, without being asked. My parents arranged it behind my back. I didn’t want the engagement, but what I wanted wasn’t important and was almost beside the point. Not The Property Of follows the years between sixteen and nineteen as I try to hold onto my education and the right to make decisions about my own life. While secretly involved with a Muslim boy, I find myself caught between religious and cultural boundaries, an unwanted fiancé and a family determined that my engagement will not end, no matter what I say.</p>
