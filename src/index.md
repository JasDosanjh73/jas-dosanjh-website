blockOne: |
 To be honest, I didn’t set out to write a book, let alone a memoir, but my friend suggested that I should write the story of my life. When I protested that nobody would be interested in or care about my life, and asked why I would want to revisit something so painful, he wasn’t having any of it. “It will help women and girls by telling your story.”

 I initially thought I could fit everything into one book, but that first book became three, written ferociously over four months. Once the first draft was done, I began turning the shite into stories people could follow, relate to, and possibly learn from.

 I wanted to take readers back inside the moments themselves and then ask the hard questions: “Why did people behave that way?” “Why did I react that way?” “Why did my family make decisions that caused so much pain?” Eventually, I wrote seven books, as once I decided to tell my story, I wanted to tell everything. The good, the bad, and the very ugly.

 I’m glad that sharing my story helps girls and women who recognise something of themselves, have been through similar experiences or are living through them now. Parts of my story are messy. There are questionable choices, terrible decisions, and embarrassing behaviour. But it’s a story that, as one reader put it, is “a story that I couldn’t stop reading, hoping and wishing that it would have a happy ending; everyone should read it.”

 So go ahead and read it. If you’re wondering whether your life will ever look different from what you’re living now, I want you to know it can.
