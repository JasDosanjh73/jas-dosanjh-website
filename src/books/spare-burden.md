What does it do to a girl to grow up believing that simply being born female has already made her a disappointment? Spare Burden, the first book in the seven-part Life Out of Reach series, follows Jas through a British-Punjabi childhood full of contradictions.

There are library books, cousins, Christmas dinners, Dallas, gurdwara adventures and a family capable of laughter, generosity and love. There is also Mama — Jas’s beloved grandmother, lost when Jas is still a toddler, yet never entirely absent from her life. But Jas and her five sisters grow up surrounded by another message: daughters are burdens and sons are something desired and prayed for. When the long-awaited boy finally arrives after six girls, the celebrations make the difference impossible to ignore.

Outside the home, Jas begins questioning other things she has inherited. At school she befriends Muslim girls despite the hostility between the Sikh and Muslim communities. Then religious rivalry becomes terrifyingly real when her uncle kills a young Muslim man, leaving consequences that ripple through the whole family. All the while, Jas is looking outwards. Books become an escape and education becomes possibility. She imagines university, journalism, travel and a future in which she chooses what happens to her.

By sixteen, she believes that future is waiting, it’s just around the corner. While Jas is dreaming and planning, she has no idea how many other people believe her future belongs to them.
