The wedding is over. And now Jas has to live the life everybody else chose for her. Searching For Breath, the third book in the Life Out of Reach series, begins on the evening of Jas’s wedding.

At nineteen, after years of resistance, she has been married off to Jay. As she crosses the threshold into his family home, the celebrations are barely over before she feels it: she is in the wrong place. Her own family has gone home. Jas cannot.

Inside the house is a hierarchy she is expected to understand quickly. Manjit has strong ideas about how a daughter-in-law should behave. Ranbir dominates the family. Cooking, serving, obedience and respect are not requests, but expectations.

And then there is Jay. The relationship is far more complicated and tangled than expected. The husband she never wanted can be affectionate, attentive and willing to challenge his parents for her. In a hostile household, he sometimes becomes her safest person. But safety and freedom are not the same thing.

Jay may defend Jas from his family, yet he also believes she belongs to him. When she desperately wants to return to Leicester, his response is gentle, but unmistakable: she isn’t going anywhere. He knows that her own parents would send her straight back to him. University becomes Jas’s breathing space: somewhere to study, think and remember that an entire world still exists beyond the house.

As family tensions deepen and the apparently respectable household begins revealing what secrets lie behind its closed doors, Jas finds herself caught between dependence and resistance, affection and possession, obedience and the immovable part of herself that refuses to be owned by anyone.

She may now be somebody’s wife. But somewhere underneath it all, she’s still Jas first and foremost.
