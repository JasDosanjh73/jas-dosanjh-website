What happens when everyone has a say in a girl’s future except the girl herself? Not The Property Of begins with sixteen-year-old Jas engaged to Jay, a boy she did not choose and has no intention of marrying. Her family sees a good match. Jas sees a decision made without her.

Then school gives her a taste of another life. There she meets Azad – a Muslim and one of the most feared boys in her school. Jas has been raised amid deep Sikh-Muslim hostility, but instead of staying away, she sends him an anonymous Valentine card and eventually, she tells him herself that she likes him. For Jas, the significance goes far beyond teenage romance.

She chose Azad. Nobody selected him for her. Nobody negotiated between families. Nobody simply handed her over without her permission - Jas acts entirely on what she wants.

But crossing that religious boundary has consequences. Sikh pupils resent seeing a Sikh girl with a Muslim boy, while her Muslim friends unexpectedly become some of her fiercest protectors. At home, discovery of the relationship brings family honour, religion and control crashing down around her. Meanwhile, the engagement to Jay remains firmly in place and Jas’s ambitions for university and journalism begin colliding with everybody else’s plans for her.

Two boys come to represent two completely different futures. One chosen for her. One chosen by her.

But Not The Property Of ultimately asks a much larger question: who does Jas belong to? And every time Jas tries to answer that question – she belongs to no one, only herself - her family and fiancé tighten their grip on her and prove otherwise.
